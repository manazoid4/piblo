Asks first

Designed to wait for your YES.

In testing: one way Piblo is designed to help.

  1. You say “Save this and remind me to finish it tonight.”
  2. Piblo shows exactly what it will do.
  3. You press YES to approve the proposed steps.
  • A real key, not a pop-up.Anything that changes your PC, sends or books is designed to wait for a press on Piblo. In our tests, an approval sent from the PC without the device was refused.
  • You see exactly what.Who, what and which file, on one screen. Ignore it and it expires.
  • Your voice stays at home.You hold a key to talk, and your words are turned into text on your own PC by default, unless you choose to add a cloud service. A hands-free wake word and a one-switch offline mode are planned, and both will be yours to turn off.

Questions

Straight answers

What works today?

A working prototype: an early handheld talking to Piblo Core on a Windows PC. Reminders, promises, focus sessions and approvals run there today. Piblo Home and the final pocket piece are being designed and printed now.

How does the £5 reservation work?

Your £5 comes off the £129 early price, so Batch 01 costs you £124 in total, and you get priority access. Changed your mind? We’ll refund it any time before your device ships: reply to your receipt or email info@piblo.co.uk.

Do I need to be good with technology?

No. Piblo is designed to be set up by asking it in plain words (in testing). If you like tinkering, the code is open too.

Is there a subscription?

No subscription is needed. Piblo Core is free and runs on your own PC. Some online extras, like train times, use the internet, and a one-switch offline mode is planned.

Does it need a computer?

Today, yes: Piblo Core runs on a Windows PC. An Android app is in development, and more platforms will follow as interest grows.

Is it always listening?

Today you hold a key to talk. A hands-free wake word is planned. It will be on by default, easy to switch off, and designed to listen for its name on your own devices.

Does it get better over time?

That’s the plan. Piblo is designed to update itself only when you allow it, and you can turn that off. Automatic updates are in testing.

Can I see the code?

Yes. It’s public on GitHub and free for personal, non-commercial use (PolyForm Noncommercial). That makes it source-available rather than open source.

When does it ship?

No date yet. We’ll set one when the hardware is proven and quotes are in.
