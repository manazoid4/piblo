WHAT IT DOES

Your agents, one workspace.

Claude Code, Codex and other command-line agents keep their own tools and chats. PibloCraft adds named panes, a shared job queue and results you can review.

01 · PANES

Pick an agent for each pane

Open three panes and choose Claude (two accounts), Codex, Hermes or any of 20 other providers, from DeepSeek and Kimi to OpenRouter and local Ollama. Each pane shows how much usage is left and stars the best one.

02 · JOBS

Give it a job, or a whole night

Type pc "Improve the README" and a free agent picks it up. For a bounded shift, add a time: for 2 hours. Work runs in separate git worktrees, and pc morning reads back what happened.

03 · STATUS

See what needs you

A status view shows each agent as working, waiting for you or idle, with its latest result. pc ? gives the short version, and pc answer walks through the decisions waiting on you, one at a time.

04 · REVIEW

Results you can check

Tasks carry completion checks, and another agent reviews the work. pc diff shows what changed in a task and pc ship commits and pushes its branch. You still review before anything merges.

WITH PIBLO

When it needs you, it tells your desk.

  1. 1

    Start the work

    Run pc, pick your agents and hand out a job.

  2. 2

    Agents ask on the board

    When one needs a decision, it lands on a shared board and in pc ?.

  3. 3

    See it on your Piblo

    An optional bridge sends messages and the morning brief to your Piblo, so you can look up from the screen.

The bridge is off until you set it up. Today it goes one way, from PibloCraft to Piblo. Sending jobs from your Piblo back to PibloCraft is planned, not built. Piblo is still a working prototype.

STAYS IN YOUR HANDS

Quiet by default. You stay in charge.

Nothing starts on its own

Agents open idle and messages wait in an inbox. Autonomous behaviour is a switch you turn on.

A guard on risky commands

It blocks pushes to main, force pushes and mass deletes for Claude. It is a safety net, not a sandbox, so keep each agent's own approvals on.

Your keys stay with you

Provider keys are saved once in a private folder on your PC, never in a repo, and only provider panes can read them.

Works on Windows with Yaw or Windows Terminal. The tmux backend for Linux, macOS and WSL has dry-run tests but no verified live session yet. You bring your own agent logins; none are included.
